The next thing: where AI is taking work (2027–2029).

By Marc Stern · 11 min read

Bottom line

The best bet for 2027–2029 is identity security for AI agents, entered through a W-2 senior solutions engineer role at an identity or cloud-security vendor.

Nothing was ruled out up front. Every option, inside or outside tech, was ranked on market demand 2027–2029 first: skilled trades and power, data center facilities, healthcare, buying or starting a business, founding a startup, and sales. Your schedule and pay situation appear as a separate column in the scorecard, not as a filter, so you can see the full field and decide which trade-offs you'd accept.

AI is flooding enterprises with automated workers, and every agent needs credentials, permissions and an owner. The identity backbone they plug into is still mostly Active Directory and Entra, which you have run at scale for 15 years. Morgan Stanley expects the enterprise identity security market to grow from about $24B to $60B in two years on this demand.

Senior sales engineer base pay runs $145K–$185K, with $200K–$270K on-target earnings. The vendor generates the leads, so your proven weak spot (self-promotion) stops being the bottleneck, and there is no on-call pager.

Keep building things, but as a portfolio that gets you hired, not as the income plan. If you want a bigger swing, the strongest outside-the-box bet is buying an existing trades or facilities business from a retiring owner, ranked second below.

Why your current ideas feel flooded

You are right: AI commoditized exactly the category you have been selling into. The rule is simple: AI devalues anything that can be generated, and raises the value of anything that must be owned.

Devalued by AI (supply exploded) Scarcer because of AI (supply can't scale)
Playbooks, guides, templates, interview prep Accountability: someone who signs off and answers to auditors or regulators
Script libraries and "copy-paste PowerShell" Access: someone trusted inside an enterprise's real environment
PDF audits and generic assessments Securing what the AI itself touches (agents, credentials, data)
Routine sysadmin work: patching, monitoring, provisioning Physical work: wiring, cooling, power, hands-on care
Solo SaaS with no distribution Distribution: a company that already owns the buyer relationship

Your own results confirm it. A year of 1099 consulting produced no revenue, and the product suite, site and trading bots are at zero. That is not a skill problem. A buyer can get a comparable script template from an AI assistant in seconds, and a solo seller with no network can't out-shout thousands of others doing the same.

Where AI is taking the workforce (2027–2029)

AI is not shrinking total work; it is churning it fast, hollowing out routine knowledge work while creating shortages in security, care and physical infrastructure.

  • Net growth, heavy churn. The WEF Future of Jobs Report 2025 projects 170M jobs created and 92M displaced by 2030, with 22% of jobs disrupted. Workers can expect 39% of their current skills to be outdated over 2025–2030.
  • Fastest-growing skills are AI and big data, then networks and cybersecurity, then technological literacy.
  • US outlook (BLS 2024–2034). Healthcare adds the most jobs because of an aging population; information security analysts grow 29% (median $124,910) and data scientists 34%. AI and automation are expected to cut 752,100 office and admin support jobs.
  • IT operations is compressing. Patching, monitoring and provisioning are being automated by cloud-native and AIOps tools. Sysadmin roles still have volume (median about $142K in one Q1 2026 Linux job sample) but are stagnating, while junior SE and ops seats are shrinking.
  • Agents are arriving faster than companies can run them safely. Gartner predicts over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027 over cost, unclear value and weak risk controls, yet expects 15% of daily work decisions to be made by agents by 2028.
  • The physical buildout is the hardest constraint. Microsoft's president called electricians the top constraint on US data center expansion in March 2026, ahead of chips and land.

The pattern: the scarce people are those who make AI safe, make it work in messy real environments, or build the physical plant it runs on.

Unmet demand 1–3 years out

Six needs grow because of AI, not despite it. Three sit in tech, three outside it.

Inside tech

  1. Securing AI agents (identity for non-humans). Every agent is a credential. Non-human identities outnumber humans 50:1 in enterprises, with projections of 80:1 within two years, and 92% of organizations say legacy IAM can't manage the risk. Morgan Stanley (Sep 2026) expects enterprise identity security to grow from about $24B to $60B over two years. Vendors are moving: SailPoint launched agent identity security, and Simeio and Clutch partnered on non-human identity in Aug 2026.
  2. AI governance and compliance. Companies are deploying AI faster than they can govern it. The EU AI Act's high-risk obligations were pushed to 2 Dec 2027 and 2 Aug 2028, which puts the compliance wave squarely in your window. In the US, FedRAMP is fast-tracking AI services through FedRAMP 20x, which shifts authorization toward automation and machine-readable evidence.
  3. Deploying AI in messy real environments. With so many agent projects failing, the people who make AI work in production are scarce. Forward-deployed engineer postings jumped over 1,000% Jan–Aug 2026 versus the prior year; Palantir's forward-deployed infrastructure track pays $135K–$200K base, and most roles carry heavy travel.

Outside tech

  1. Power and the physical AI plant. Dallas–Fort Worth has 4,587 MW of operating data center capacity and 3,202 MW under construction, ranked the largest in the world in 2026. Nationally, about 340,000 data center roles may go unfilled. Critical facilities managers average about $168K, and an Allen, TX facilities manager role listed $120K–$150K base plus bonus. Licensed electricians take 4–5 years to train.
  2. The ownership transfer of Main Street. McKinsey sees about $5T of baby boomer businesses coming up for sale over the next decade, yet 92% of small-business exits today end in closure. Buyers typically use SBA 7(a) loans (up to $5M) plus seller notes. Trades and facilities businesses (HVAC, electrical, fire and life safety) sit right on top of the AI buildout's labor shortage.
  3. Care for an aging population. Healthcare adds the most US jobs through 2034; nurse practitioners grow 40% (median $129,210) and physician assistants 20%. Demand is certain, but the training path is 3–6 years.

Two things AI made worse rather than better: generic content and junior-level IT. Avoid building a plan on either.

Two patterns stand out. The top three all reuse 15 years of experience instead of resetting it. The outside-tech options with the most certain demand (electrician, nurse practitioner) are the ones that throw away the most of what you already have.

Recommended path

Primary: become a senior solutions engineer for identity and AI-agent security. Parallel long play: start searching for a trades or facilities business to buy.

Why the primary fits you specifically

  • Active Directory is where agent sprawl lives. Service accounts, SPNs, gMSAs, Entra app registrations and service principals are the non-human identities vendors are now trying to govern. You have managed them across thousands of servers.
  • FedRAMP and sovereign cloud are rare. Identity vendors selling to federal, EU, UK and AU buyers need engineers who can talk authorization boundaries and data residency credibly.
  • You have built agent tooling. Your InfraTools MCP server automates AD unlocks and resets, which is exactly the agent-touches-identity problem these buyers fear. Few SE candidates can demo that.
  • The vendor solves distribution. An SE partners with an account executive who brings the pipeline. You do the part you're strong at: technical discovery, demos, proofs of value.

For a first SE role, an established platform is safer; Morgan Stanley flags consolidation across identity vendors, which means startup acquisitions and reshuffles.

What it pays

Per KORE1's 2026 guide, cybersecurity senior SEs earn $160K–$195K base and $230K–$310K OTE; remote roles average $135K–$170K base. Realized pay is typically about 75% of OTE because quota attainment has run 65–75%. Even the remote base floor is above your current pay.

Screen every offer for

  • Travel percentage, in writing
  • Territory time zone (a West territory means late calls; East means early)
  • Who controls your calendar, and whether afternoon blocks are protected
  • First-year ramp guarantee on variable pay

The parallel long play: buy a business

This is the real outside-the-box option, and it pairs well with a W-2 role because searching can happen while employed. Retiring owners of HVAC, electrical, fire and life safety, or low-voltage businesses sit on top of the same labor shortage driving the AI buildout. Your edge would be running operations with modern systems and automation in companies that have none. It needs capital and a personal guarantee, so treat it as a 2028+ move funded by the higher salary.

Fallback

If SE interviews don't come within about 90 days, pivot to AI governance or compliance automation engineering. It's steadier (no quota) and leans on your FedRAMP experience.

Training plan

About 2 hours a day for 6–9 months gets you interview-ready; the rest of your side-project time goes to applications and the portfolio. Certificates matter less than a working demo you can show.

Phase 1 — Nov 2026 to Jan 2027: build the proof

Phase 2 — Feb to Apr 2027: credentials and pipeline

Phase 3 — May to Sep 2027: land and expand

Risks, what would change the call, and this week

The biggest risk isn't picking the wrong field; it's spreading 4–5 hours a day across five side projects again. Pick one primary track and one long play.
